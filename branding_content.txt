MY STORY
I grew up different.
I learned to live fully anyway.
I was born with dwarfism.
Growing up in India meant learning early that not every space would be designed for someone like me.
Sometimes it was a physical challenge.
Sometimes it was people's perception.
Sometimes it was simply figuring out how to do an ordinary thing in a slightly different way.
But I never wanted my life to become a collection of limitations.
So I kept moving.
School.
College.
Work.
Higher education.
New environments.
New people.
New challenges.
One step at a time.
Not because every step was easy.
Because the next step was still worth taking.
 
SCHOOL
My first lesson wasn't about success.
It was about belonging.
School taught me that being different can make you noticeable before you've even had the opportunity to introduce yourself.
There were questions.
There were assumptions.
There were moments when I had to navigate things differently from everyone else.
But there were also friendships, teachers, learning and experiences that shaped who I became.
I completed my schooling.
And somewhere along the way, I learned an important lesson:
You don't need everyone to understand you before you start believing in yourself.
 
COLLEGE
Then came commerce.
I pursued my B.Com at Madura College.
College expanded my world.
It introduced me to business, people, ideas and possibilities that were much bigger than the environment I had grown up in.
But I also discovered something else.
I enjoyed understanding people.
Why they think.
Why they connect.
Why they remember certain stories.
Why some ideas move people while others disappear.
That curiosity eventually led me towards marketing, communication and creativity.
 
PGDM
Then I chose to go further.
I pursued my PGDM in Marketing at Thiagarajar School of Management.
Business school challenged me in a different way.
Presentations.
Group discussions.
Projects.
Deadlines.
Competition.
Networking.
New environments.
It wasn't simply about earning another qualification.
It was about discovering what I was capable of when I put myself in rooms where I wasn't necessarily the obvious person to notice.
And I learned:
**Confidence isn't something you wait for.
It is something you build by showing up.**
 
THE CAREER CHAPTER
I started by solving business problems.
My professional journey began in the world of enterprise technology and business solutioning.
At HCLTech, I worked across bid management and solutioning, translating complex technology into stories that clients could understand.
I worked on $15M+ in application-led RFx responses, developed executive narratives, supported client workshops and worked with complex commercial models.
It taught me something that would become central to my career:
Every complex idea needs a human story.
Technology has a story.
A brand has a story.
A business has a story.
And people have stories.
My job became learning how to tell them.

DWARFISM & MY INDIA
I don't want to tell a pity story.
Because my life isn't one.
Being a person with dwarfism in India has meant experiencing both progress and barriers.
There are laws.
There are accessibility initiatives.
There are organisations working towards inclusion.
There are more conversations around disability than there were before.
But everyday life can still reveal a different reality.
A building may have accessibility standards and still be difficult to navigate.
A workplace may talk about inclusion while people still carry assumptions.
A public space may technically be accessible but not practically comfortable.
And sometimes the biggest barrier isn't physical.
It is perception.
Being seen as a person before being seen as a disability.
That matters.
Because accessibility isn't only about infrastructure.
It is also about attitude, opportunity and expectation.
 
WHAT DWARFISM GAVE ME
A different way of seeing.
Dwarfism changed the way I experience the world.
It made me notice things.
The height of a counter.
The design of a stage.
The accessibility of a building.
The way people look.
The assumptions people make.
The small things most people never have to think about.
And that perspective became useful far beyond accessibility.
It taught me to question the default.
To notice the overlooked.
To think from another person's point of view.
To understand that two people can stand in the same place and experience completely different worlds.
That's not a weakness.
That's perspective.
